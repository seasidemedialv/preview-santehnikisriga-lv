Wooden houses from the 1820s and the groundwater beneath them

In 1812 the wooden buildings of Zasulauks were damaged, but as early as the 1820s new wooden houses began to be built, and many of them can still be seen. The residential areas here include multi-storey and low-rise buildings as well as detached houses.

In a two-hundred-year-old building the water supply and sewerage were added later, and one house often has steel, copper and plastic pipes from different repairs. The joint between two materials is a typical leak point. So when repairing, it is worth replacing the whole section up to the nearest sound joint, not just the damaged piece.

In Zasulauks the groundwater mostly lies at 1.5–3 m. For basements this means less damp than in the low coastal districts, but moisture still builds up in the foundations and crawl space of a wooden house if a leaking pipe goes unnoticed. A puddle in a dry basement more often points to a damaged pipe than to groundwater.

A water heater (boiler) on a timber wall

If hot water in a wooden house comes from an electric water heater, its weight must be taken into account: an 80–100 litre water heater full of water weighs more than 100 kg, and a timber partition or cladding will not hold it safely. It has to be fixed to the load-bearing structure or on a separate frame.

The water heater needs a safety valve with an outlet to the sewer, so that drips from the valve do not fall on the wooden floor. Water heater installation starts from €45; the price depends on the type of fixing, the length of the supply pipes and whether the old water heater has to be removed.

A bathroom in a wooden house

In a wooden house the biggest risk in a bathroom is water getting under the finish. If the floor and walls around the shower cabin or bath have no waterproofing, seepage from a joint or floor drain goes unnoticed for a long time and damages the timber structure.

So when renovating a bathroom, the plumbing has to be planned together with the waterproofing: drain falls, the level of the floor drain and the points where pipes pass through the wall. Bathroom plumbing starts from €200, shower cabin installation from €80.

1970s–1980s estates: the pipes are past their service life

The housing estates in Zasulauks were built in the 1970s and 1980s. Annex 2 of Cabinet Regulation No. 906 (LV portāls summary) sets a service life of 15–30 years for cold water pipes and sewer pipes depending on type, and 60 years for cast-iron sewers. If the building still has its original water risers and supply pipes, their service life has been exceeded.

Replacement of the risers themselves is organised by the building, but in an apartment the supply pipes up to the riser can be replaced. Riser replacement starts from €100 per riser within one floor, labour only.

Pipe service life: Annex 2 of Cabinet Regulation No. 906, LV portāls summary. Checked 05.11.2026.

A new inlet for a house with several owners

If an old wooden house belongs to several owners and needs a new connection or a rebuilt inlet, any one of the co-owners can request technical conditions from Rīgas ūdens, but the design has to be agreed with all of them. Technical conditions are valid for one year.

So before the design work it is worth agreeing who organises the job and how the costs are shared. Inside the house each apartment needs its own stop valve, so that a repair in one apartment does not leave all the others without water.
