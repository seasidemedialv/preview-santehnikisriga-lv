Few houses over a large area

Kleisti is one of Riga's largest and least populated districts. Mixed-use areas take up 17.4% and lie along the planned Piejūras maģistrāle (Coastal Highway) and the Northern Corridor. The houses are scattered, and the distances between them are large.

For a detached house in a place like this, the plumbing can be completely off-grid: water from its own well or borehole and waste water in its own system. Whether that is also the case in your house depends on the specific property, not the district as a whole.

Is there a city network in your street?

Rīgas ūdens has not published its network coverage for Kleisti. If you want to connect or build a new house, you need to request technical conditions from Rīgas ūdens. They can be requested by the developer or one of the co-owners, and Rīgas ūdens prepares them within 11 working days free of charge. The conditions show whether and where a connection is possible.

Until there is a connection, the plumbing work in the house is the same as in any off-grid house: the pump, the pressure tank, the water heater (boiler) and the internal sewerage up to the outlet.

Local sewerage and Riga Regulation No. 66

In Kleisti too, Riga Binding Regulation No. 66 applies to a septic tank, holding tank or local treatment plant. The system must be registered with the Housing and Environment Department: for existing systems the deadline was 31.12.2021, for new ones it is one month after construction.

We work on the inside of the house: blockages, smells and pipes up to the outlet. Pumping out the tank is not our job.

Technical conditions: Cabinet Regulation No. 174, paragraph 19 (Rīgas ūdens). Registration of local systems: Riga Binding Regulation No. 66 (likumi.lv). Network coverage in Kleisti is not publicly described. Checked 05.11.2026.

The Hapaka ditch and the dune ridge: two different places

Around the Hapaka ditch and in the Spilve meadows groundwater is shallower than 1.5 m, and the flood risk is up to 10%, and even higher if the hydraulic structures are not maintained. The Bolderāja–Priedaine dune ridge, on the other hand, has two parallel dunes 4–10 m high.

  • In the low-lying places a sump pump is useful for a basement or plant room, and a septic tank or holding tank in high groundwater needs a watertight tank.
  • In the dune area the ground is sandy, and a water inlet laid shallow freezes in winter; thawing from €55.
  • If water comes from a borehole, pump and pressure tank repair costs from €80.

Water from your own well: the system and typical faults

In an off-grid house the water supply is the house's own small waterworks: a pump in the well or borehole, a pressure tank, a pressure switch and filters. If any part fails, the house is left without water, so it is worth knowing what the typical faults look like.

  • The pump switches on often and for short bursts: the air pressure in the pressure tank has dropped or the membrane is damaged.
  • The pump runs but no water comes: a clogged filter, a low water level in the well or a leak in the suction pipe.
  • The pressure slowly drops at night when nobody is using water: there is a leak somewhere in the system or the non-return valve does not hold.

Before the repair we find out whether the pump has to be lifted out of the borehole, because that affects the price most.
