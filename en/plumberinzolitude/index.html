Plumber in Zolitūde, Riga

A plumber in Zolitūde works in one of the newest Soviet-era housing estates, yet its buildings are already more than 40 years old too. In the north, 119 series multi-storey blocks dominate, while the centre and south of the district have detached houses. Here we describe how the work differs between the two parts and what shallow groundwater means.

A housing estate started in 1983

Construction of Zolitūde began in 1983. The residential estate in the north of the district is dominated by 119 series buildings with 6–10 storeys and 16-storey tower blocks. In a tall building one leak can reach many floors below, so a quick shut-off of the water matters most here.

If water is leaking from your apartment, close the inlet valve and warn the neighbours below. If it is coming from above and you cannot get into the apartment above you, report it to the house manager at once so that the riser is shut off. After that we help with what is in your apartment: water leak repair costs from €45.

In tower blocks and 10-storey buildings the sewer riser collects waste water from many apartments. If it blocks in the lower part, water first rises in the baths and toilets on the lowest floors, not in the apartment where the blockage started. So in the case of such a blockage, report it to the house manager first, and only then clean the apartment's own pipe.

119 series: 60 years for the building, far less for the pipes

Annex 2 of Cabinet Regulation No. 906 places the 119 series in group 4, which has a set service life of 60 years. Cold water pipes and sewer pipes are given 15–30 years depending on type, and sewers with cast-iron fittings 60 years.

Zolitūde buildings, built in the mid-1980s, have exceeded most of these service lives for pipes. If an apartment still has its first steel supply pipes, they rust from the inside, and the pressure at the taps drops gradually. Before renovating a bathroom it is worth finding out whether the building plans riser replacement (from €100 per riser within one floor).

Water pressure in a 16-storey building tends to vary between floors. If it is weak only in your apartment, the cause is often a clogged valve or filter at the inlet, which can be found with low water pressure diagnostics (from €25).

Service lives: Annex 2 of Cabinet Regulation No. 906 (LV portāls summary, 2019). Checked 05.11.2026.

Detached houses in central and southern Zolitūde

Detached housing takes up 19% of the district, or 56 ha, and dominates the central and southern parts. There are no common property risers here, but each house has its own inlet pipe from the street.

Rīgas ūdens provides the service up to the ownership boundary. Most often this is the outer edge of the wall of the meter chamber, or, if there is no chamber, the street's red line. Everything beyond this boundary is the owner's responsibility, including leaks in the garden.

If damp appears in the meter chamber or in one spot on the lawn, first find out which side of the boundary it is on. An inlet pipe that runs shallow or is not insulated can freeze in winter; thawing frozen pipes costs from €55.

Ownership boundary: Rīgas ūdens, responsibility boundaries for water services. Checked 05.11.2026.

Shallow groundwater and bog deposits

In Zolitūde the groundwater is mostly shallower than 1.5 m. The most favourable building conditions are in the north-western part, where there are no bog deposits; elsewhere they occur.

  • Water may appear in the basement of a detached house every spring. A sump pit with an automatic pump is the simplest solution; sump pump repair from €75.
  • A sewer pipe in soft ground needs a stable bed, otherwise it settles over time and the fall disappears.
  • In the basement of a block of apartments, damp can come from the ground or from the bottom of a leaking riser. Look at the pipes before you look for the cause in the ground.

Zolitūde is named in the title of the Rīgas ūdens project "Water Management Development in Riga, Phase 3" (2006–2009). The Rīgas ūdens page does not list exactly what work was done in Zolitūde, so ask them directly about the state of the city network at your address.
