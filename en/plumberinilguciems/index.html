Plumber in Iļģuciems, Riga

A plumber in Iļģuciems mostly works in buildings put up in the 1950s and 1960s: the first standard-design buildings and the high-rise microdistricts built later. If the pipes in these buildings have not been replaced, their standard service life ended long ago. Here we explain what this means when something leaks, blocks or the bathroom needs renovating.

From factory workers' houses to three microdistricts

The history of Iļģuciems records that in the 1950s the first standard-design buildings, including 303 series houses, were built for workers of the cement and slate factory. In the 1960s a high-rise residential district grew up around them, and the 1968 plan provided for three microdistricts. Residential development now takes up 43.4% of the district (about 106 ha), mostly multi-storey apartment buildings.

For a plumber this means the age of the building tells you more here than the street. In a 1950s building and a 1960s microdistrict block the risers may be laid out differently, and every apartment bears the marks of earlier renovations. So before giving a price we look at what is inside the wall and how the transition to the riser was made.

Pipe life is shorter than building life

Annex 2 of Cabinet Regulation No. 906 (summary by LV portāls) sets a service life of 15–30 years for cold water pipes and sewer pipes, depending on the type. Only sewerage with cast iron fittings lasts longer, 60 years. So in a building put up more than 60 years ago, the original pipes have outlived several standard periods.

Signs that the pipes are worn out: rusty water for the first few seconds after standing overnight, sweating joints with rust stains in the toilet, seepage at the threads. This is not an emergency yet, but it is time to plan replacement before the water reaches the neighbours.

Full replacement of the water and sewer branch pipes in an apartment from the risers (bathroom and the branch to the kitchen) starts from €500 for labour. The price depends on the number of connection points, exposed or concealed installation and removal of the old material. A single damaged section can be repaired: pipe repair from €35.

Service life: Annex 2 of Cabinet Regulation No. 906, summary by LV portāls. Checked 05.11.2026.

The boundary between your pipes and the building's pipes

Section 3(2) of the Apartment Property Law (Dzīvokļa īpašuma likums) states that an apartment includes the utility lines up to the common property risers, and Section 4(1) that the systems serving the building are common property. In an Iļģuciems building this means: the branch from the riser to the tap, the toilet, the bath and the traps are yours, but the riser that runs through all the floors belongs to all the owners together.

Often the branch pipes in an apartment are replaced but the transition to the old riser is left, and then that transition becomes the weakest point. If riser replacement is being discussed in the building, it is better value to time the apartment work to match. How the costs are decided is explained in the article who pays for riser replacement.

Boundary between the apartment and common property: Apartment Property Law, Sections 3 and 4 (likumi.lv). Checked 05.11.2026.

Ground: good in most places, weak at the edges

In most of Iļģuciems the ground is sandy, groundwater is 1.5–3 m deep, and there is practically no flood risk. On the northern and eastern edges the ground is unstable, with organic content.

This means little for apartment plumbing, but a lot for the building's outlet in the yard. In weak ground the pipe can gradually settle and lose its fall; then blockages keep recurring on the ground floor or in the basement, even though everything drains on the upper floors. In that case the building's network is to blame, and it has to be dealt with through the house manager.

A toilet or bath in a building with cast iron sewerage

When replacing a toilet or bath in a 1950s–1960s building, a cast iron outlet usually turns up. The transition from cast iron to plastic must be made with a suitable seal, and if the edge of the old pipe has crumbled, its end must also be replaced. Otherwise the new toilet starts seeping within the first month.

Toilet installation and replacement starts from €60, bath replacement from €80, and removal of the old bath from €30. The price goes up if the outlet has to be rebuilt, the floor is uneven or a cast iron bath has to be broken up to carry it out through a narrow stairwell.
