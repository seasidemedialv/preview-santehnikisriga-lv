Why it pays for property managers to work with us long-term

A single building means dozens of apartments and hundreds of potential plumbing issues a year - from small mixer tap repairs to riser failures affecting several floors at once. If every problem means finding a new plumber, that means longer wait times, unclear pricing, and unhappy tenants complaining to the property manager. When one service provider looks after a building long-term, it is easier to recognise recurring problems and judge which call-out is an emergency and which can wait for the next scheduled visit. That means less time spent on coordination and fewer tenant complaints for you.

Plumber for an apartment owners' association

An association that manages its building itself is responsible for the same things as a house manager: inspecting the utility systems, technical maintenance and routine repairs. In plumbing this means regularly checking the basement, the horizontal mains, the riser valves and the building inlet, rather than waiting for an emergency.

  • Scheduled inspection: leaks in the basement, rust on valves, the condition of the horizontal sewer mains, access hatches.
  • Emergency work: a leaking riser, a blocked horizontal main, a burst valve.
  • Larger jobs: replacing the risers in the whole building in stages.

Replacing the risers across the building needs a decision of the owners' meeting on the work and its financing, because the risers are common property. The work is planned riser by riser, so each apartment is without water for as short a time as possible, and residents are warned in advance. Riser replacement starts from €100 per riser within one floor, and work for associations from €30.

Sources: Section 6(2) of the Residential Property Management Law (Dzīvojamo māju pārvaldīšanas likums) (likumi.lv); LV portāls e-consultations of 24.10.2024 and 09.04.2025 on risers as common property. Checked 05.11.2026.
