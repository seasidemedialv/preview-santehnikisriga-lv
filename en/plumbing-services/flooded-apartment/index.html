What to do in the FIRST MINUTES

A flooded apartment is one of the most stressful situations at home. What you do in the first few minutes can decide whether the damage stays small or spreads down through several floors and neighbours.

1

Shut off the water IMMEDIATELY

Close the main water shut-off valve in your apartment. If you can't find it or can't manage, at least shut off the specific appliance or tap that's leaking.

2

Protect electrical appliances

Unplug or move electrical appliances away from the water. If water has reached outlets, switch off the relevant circuit at the fuse box - don't step into water if you're not sure it's safe.

3

Document it and book us

Photograph the flooded areas - useful for disputes or insurance claims. Then fill in the form right away - we contact you within 5 minutes and agree a time.

Safety first: If water is close to the fuse box or electrical outlets and you're not sure you can act safely - don't do anything yourself. Wait for our plumber, or if the situation feels dangerous, contact the emergency services at 112.

Why every minute matters

A flood is one of the rare plumbing situations where time directly determines the extent of the damage. The first 15-30 minutes usually decide whether the water stays in your room or spreads to the neighbours below, creating disputes and extra costs. Standing water on flooring or parquet can cause irreversible damage within just a couple of hours, and moisture that soaks into walls can lead to mould later on. That's why we treat flooding call-outs as our highest priority - our plumber tries to come as soon as possible, also nights and weekends, equipped to quickly locate and stop the water rather than just assess the situation from the sidelines.

Checking the plumbing after a flood

Once the water has been stopped and the floor dried, you need to make sure the leak will not happen again and that there is no second, hidden one. After a flood, these are usually checked:

  • the joint that leaked: has it been replaced, not just tightened;
  • the flexible hoses to the mixer taps, the toilet cistern, the washing machine and the dishwasher, because they are the same age and wear out at the same time;
  • the shut-off valves: do they close all the way;
  • the traps and the machine outlets under the sinks;
  • the visible pipes and their joints with the riser.

If the water came from above, check your own apartment as well: water may have got into the boxing around the riser and under the bath. Take dated photos of damp patches and watch whether they grow.
