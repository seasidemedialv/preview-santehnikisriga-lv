Why a trap smells or leaks

A trap is a bent pipe with a water seal. The water that stays in the bend stops sewer air from entering the room. If the drain smells, the water seal is usually gone or the trap is no longer airtight.

  • Dried-out water seal: the sink or shower has not been used for several weeks, and the water in the bend has evaporated. In this case running the water is enough; the trap does not need replacing.
  • Water sucked out of the trap: when a lot of water is let out upstairs, a vacuum forms in the riser and pulls the water out of the bend. This usually comes with gurgling.
  • A loose flexible pipe or seal in the drain opening: air gets past the trap through the gap.
  • Deposits inside the trap: grease, hair and soap build-up start to smell on their own.

Leaks have other causes. Most often it is a cracked plastic body or nut, a worn cone seal at the sink waste, a stretched or kinked flexible pipe, or a trap of the wrong diameter fitted to the drain with a makeshift adapter.

If the smell remains even after replacing the trap, the cause may be in the riser or the drain ventilation. Then see the sewage smell page.

Sink, bath, shower and washing machine traps

  • Sink trap: bottle or tubular, accessed under the sink or in the vanity unit. This is the quickest job.
  • Kitchen sink trap: often for two bowls and with an overflow. Grease collects in it, so an old trap clogs up inside the fastest.
  • Bath trap: comes with the overflow, and to reach it the bath panel has to be removed. An automatic (click-clack) trap is replaced as a set with the overflow handle.
  • Shower tray trap: low, with a large waste. On some trays it can only be reached by removing the front panel or lifting the tray.
  • Trap with a branch for a washing machine or dishwasher: there is a spigot on the side where the machine's drain hose is connected. The hose must not simply be pushed into the drain pipe, because the smell then comes out through the gap.

For connecting the machine to the water supply and moving it, see the washing machine and dishwasher connection page. If you are replacing the whole sink, not just the trap, sink installation is the better fit.

How trap replacement works

  1. 1

    Checking the cause

    We find out whether the trap itself is leaking, the joint with the sink or the transition to the drain, and whether the drain is blocked further down the pipe.

  2. 2

    Removing the old trap

    The old trap is unscrewed, the old seal or flexible pipe is taken out of the drain opening, and the waste and opening are cleaned.

  3. 3

    Matching the sizes

    The new trap has to match the sink or bath waste and the drain pipe, usually 40 or 50 mm. If the sizes do not match, a factory adapter is used, not tape or silicone.

  4. 4

    Installation

    The new trap is fitted with new seals and, if needed, a branch for the machine. Plastic nuts are tightened by hand so the body does not crack.

  5. 5

    Testing with water

    The sink or bath is filled and emptied at full flow, so the joints are checked under load.

  • where the trap is: a sink trap is quicker, for a bath or shower you have to get under the bath or tray;
  • an old trap that is stuck or corroded onto the drain pipe;
  • whether the flexible pipe or the adapter to the drain also needs replacing;

The price does not include the trap itself and adapters, removing and restoring a tiled bath panel, or clearing a blockage further down the pipe. If the water still does not drain with a new trap, you need drain cleaning.
