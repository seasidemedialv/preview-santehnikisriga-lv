What matters when replacing a toilet

Most problems in a toilet replacement come not from the toilet itself but from the connection to the drain. The new toilet has to fit the existing outlet, and that has to be worked out before you buy.

  • Outlet direction: horizontal (into the wall), vertical (into the floor) or angled. The toilet needs the same outlet, otherwise an adapter or a flexible pan connector is needed.
  • The distance from the wall to the outlet and from the wall to the toilet's fixing screws.
  • The cistern supply: after years the old valve and hose often have to be replaced together with the toilet.
  • The floor under the toilet: if it is uneven or the tiles are damaged, the toilet has to be levelled so it does not wobble and the joint does not crack.

The toilet, bath, sink and the pipes after the inlet valve are the apartment owner's separate property, so an ordinary toilet replacement does not need the house manager's permission. If the drain branch at the riser has to be rebuilt, that already affects common property and has to be agreed.

Source: Section 3, Paragraph 2 of the Apartment Property Law (Dzīvokļa īpašuma likums): the apartment includes the utility networks up to the common property risers and the toilet, shower and bath fittings, likumi.lv. Checked 05.11.2026.

How a toilet replacement works

  1. 1

    Shutting off the water

    The valve at the cistern or at the apartment inlet is closed, the cistern is emptied and the water is removed from the bowl.

  2. 2

    Removing the old toilet

    The cistern and fixings are unscrewed and the toilet is disconnected from the outlet. Old toilets are often cemented in, so they are freed carefully so as not to damage the tiles and the outlet.

  3. 3

    Preparing the outlet

    The drain outlet is cleaned, its condition is checked, and a new seal or a flexible pan connector is fitted if the outlet does not fit directly.

  4. 4

    Installing the new toilet

    The toilet is positioned, levelled and fixed to the floor, and the cistern and seat are fitted.

  5. 5

    Connection and testing

    The water supply is connected, the toilet is flushed several times and we check for leaks at the outlet, the cistern and the supply.

Wall-hung toilet (concealed frame) installation

With a wall-hung toilet the cistern is hidden in the wall, and the toilet hangs on a metal concealed frame. The frame takes the whole load, so it is fixed to the floor and the wall, and only after that is the wall clad with plasterboard and tiled.

The work differs depending on the situation. If a floor-standing toilet is replaced with a wall-hung one, the drain outlet has to be rebuilt to the frame level and the water supply moved. If the frame is already built in and the wall tiled, only the toilet and the button need to go on, and that is cheaper.

Before the wall is closed we test the joints for leaks, because after tiling the cistern can only be reached through the button opening. At the same time a bidet shower can be connected, if a water supply with a mixer tap is planned next to the toilet.

Repair or replacement?

If the toilet is sound but the cistern leaks, does not stop the water or does not flush, there is no need to replace the toilet. Toilet and cistern repair is enough, and it costs from €30. Replacement makes sense if the toilet is cracked, leaks at the outlet, is very old or is being replaced together with the whole bathroom.

If water does not drain from the toilet, the problem is usually in the drain, not the toilet: see drain cleaning. If you are replacing all the bathroom fittings, see also bath replacement and sink installation, and for all jobs the plumbing services page.

Installing and replacing a concealed frame

The concealed frame is fixed to the floor and to a load-bearing wall or a plasterboard stud, and its height is adjusted so the toilet sits at a comfortable height. The waste outlet is connected to the frame pipe with a fall. The water supply goes to the cistern through a shut-off valve that stays reachable through the flush plate opening.

Before the wall is boarded, the cistern is filled and the joints are checked. After tiling, the toilet is hung and the flush plate fitted.

If an old frame is damaged or its cistern has cracked, the frame is replaced, which means opening the wall and the tiles. Often the frame is fine and only the fill valve or flush mechanism is faulty, and these are replaced through the flush plate opening without breaking the wall: concealed cistern repair. If the toilet is moving to another spot, see plumbing rough-in.
