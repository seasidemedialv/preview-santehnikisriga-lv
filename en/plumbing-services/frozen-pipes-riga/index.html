How to recognise frozen pipes

During Latvia's cold winters, frozen pipes are a common problem - especially in older buildings, houses with exterior wall pipes and unheated spaces. Here's what to look for.

No water or just a trickle

The most common sign - you turn on the tap but nothing comes out, or only a very weak flow

Pipe is cold or frosted

Visible frost on the pipe surface, or it feels significantly colder than the surroundings

Only air from the tap

The pipe is completely frozen - water cannot flow at all

Pipes in exterior walls or basement

Uninsulated pipes in exterior walls, basements or unheated verandas are most at risk

Temperature drops below -10°C

Risk increases significantly during severe cold, especially if heating has been interrupted

Clicking sounds in pipes

Audible clicking or crackling in the pipes may indicate the early stages of freezing

What to do if your pipes are frozen

Taking the right steps can prevent a much more serious problem - a burst pipe that causes flooding.

1

Shut off the main water valve

This reduces pressure on the frozen pipe and reduces the risk of a burst when the pipe starts to thaw.

!

Never use an open flame

Do not use gas torches, candles or any open flame. This can cause the pipe to burst and start a fire.

2

Book us immediately

Our plumber uses safe thawing methods and checks whether the pipe has sustained any damage.

3

Warm the room if possible

If the frozen pipe is in a heated space, raise the temperature. For pipes in basements or verandas - wait for the plumber.

Installing an outdoor tap that does not freeze

An outdoor or garden tap on a private house freezes more often than any other pipe, because its supply runs through the outer wall. If water stays in the pipe inside the wall over winter, it freezes and the pipe bursts, often inside the wall, where the leak is only noticed in spring.

There are two safe solutions. The first is a frost-proof tap: its valve sits on the warm side of the wall, and when you turn the tap off, the water drains out of the outer part by itself. The second is an ordinary outdoor tap with a shut-off valve and a drain valve inside the house, which you close and drain in autumn.

  • The supply through the wall is laid with a slight fall towards the outside, so that water drains out of it.
  • Disconnect the garden hose before winter, otherwise a frost-proof tap cannot drain.
  • Put the inside shut-off valve where it is easy to reach.

We work on the house side: the supply, the valve, the hole through the wall and the tap. We agree the price before the work. For the whole summer house system, see winterising a summer house water system.

Draining the water system for winter

If a house or summer house is not heated in winter, the safest way to avoid frozen pipes is to drain the water system before the frost. Water left in the pipes expands as it freezes and can split pipes, mixer taps and the water heater (boiler).

  1. 1

    Shut off the supply

    Close the main valve, or disconnect the well pump and the pressure tank from the power.

  2. 2

    Open all taps

    Open the hot and cold water on every tap and shower so that air can enter the system.

  3. 3

    Drain the water heater

    First switch off the power, then drain the tank.

  4. 4

    Drain the lowest points

    Open the drain cocks; if there are none, blow the water out of the pipes with a compressor.

  5. 5

    Tanks and traps

    Empty the toilet cistern, the pressure tank and the washing machine hoses. Pour antifreeze into the traps so they do not freeze and no smell rises.

In spring, refill the system slowly with the drain cocks closed, and check the joints before switching on the water heater. If the house has a water-based heating system, it is drained or filled with antifreeze separately. If the well pump also needs preparing, see well pump repair.
