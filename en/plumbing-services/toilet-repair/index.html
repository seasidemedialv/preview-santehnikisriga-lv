Cistern replacement

If the cistern runs or drips constantly, there is usually no need to replace the whole toilet. It is enough to replace the mechanism inside the cistern: the fill valve that lets water in, and the flush valve with the button that lets it out. Universal mechanisms fit most cisterns, but the button opening and the cistern lid have to match.

The whole cistern is replaced if it is cracked or parts are no longer available for it. On a close-coupled toilet the cistern is fixed to the bowl with bolts and a seal, and the new cistern has to fit that particular toilet. If none is available, it is often cheaper to replace the whole toilet.

After replacement the water level in the cistern is adjusted and we check that the button flushes in both modes. Toilet repair starts from €30, parts extra. For a cistern hidden in the wall, see concealed cistern repair.

Toilet rocking or leaking at the floor

If the toilet rocks, its connection to the drain eventually cracks and water starts leaking onto the floor behind the toilet, often unnoticed under the tiles. That is why a rocking toilet is worth fixing in good time.

  1. 1

    Cause

    Loose fixing screws, rotten plugs or an uneven floor under the toilet.

  2. 2

    Securing

    The toilet is removed, the base is cleaned, new plugs and screws are fitted, the unevenness is levelled out and the toilet is set level.

  3. 3

    Pan connector or corrugated pipe

    If the connection to the outlet has already leaked, the pan connector or corrugated pipe is replaced.

  4. 4

    Testing

    It is flushed several times and we check that it is dry behind the toilet.

If the toilet has a crack in its base, securing it won't help and the toilet has to be replaced: toilet installation, from €60.

Water leaking at the toilet: where is it coming from

If water is leaking at the toilet, first find out where it goes: down the inside of the bowl, onto the floor or down the outside of the cistern. The repair depends on that.

A simple test: put a few drops of food colouring into the cistern and don't flush for half an hour. If the colour appears in the bowl, the flush valve is leaking. If only the outside of the cistern is wet, it may be condensation, not a leak.

If water is running onto the floor, close the valve at the cistern. If the toilet is old or cracked, it is better value to install a new one: toilet installation.

Toilet cistern repair

The cistern (tank) is the heart of the mechanism: it holds the float, the fill valve and the flush mechanism. When the cistern is full but water keeps running, or the flush is weak and does not clear the bowl fully, the problem is almost always inside the cistern, not in the toilet itself.

  • Adjusting the float and valve: an incorrectly set water level is the most common reason for a weak flush or constant running
  • Replacing the flush mechanism: if the parts are worn, we replace the whole mechanism, and the replacement suits most cistern models
  • Repairing a dual-flush button: when the button is stuck or one of the two flush settings does not work
  • Repairing a built-in (concealed) cistern: mechanisms built into the wall need a special approach and spare parts

Cistern repair usually takes 20–40 minutes and does not require removing the toilet. The price starts from €25, depending on whether it needs adjustment or parts replaced.

Also part of toilet repair

  • Leak repair: sealing the joints between the cistern and the toilet
  • Water connection repair: the tap and hose at the cistern
  • Drain check: checking for blockages at the toilet's drain connection

Toilet repair is also linked to the drain system, so when needed we check the drain pipes as well.
