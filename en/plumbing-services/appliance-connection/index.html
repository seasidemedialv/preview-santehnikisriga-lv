Washing machine connection

A washing machine needs connecting after buying a new appliance, following a bathroom or kitchen renovation, or after moving into a new apartment. The job sounds simple, but it involves several steps where a mistake can cause a leak: fitting or checking a proper shut-off valve, connecting the water supply hose without over- or under-tightening it, and securely fastening the drain hose to a trap or a dedicated drain pipe so dirty water can't be siphoned back in.

Common mistake: An old or corroded shut-off valve that seems to work fine but can start leaking the moment it's closed for the first time - we check this on every connection.

Dishwasher connection

Connecting a dishwasher in the kitchen often takes more planning than a washing machine - the space is tighter, and the appliance usually sits further from the main water point under the sink. We fit a branch off the sink's water supply, run a secure drain hose, and make sure the whole connection path stays accessible for future servicing rather than sealed behind a fixed kitchen panel.

Why leave the connection to a professional

Connecting an appliance looks simple - attach a hose and switch it on. In reality, most household appliance leaks we see on emergency call-outs come from exactly this: an incorrectly attached hose or a worn shut-off valve that shows no sign of trouble for a long time, then suddenly starts leaking under the floor - somewhere it goes unnoticed and causes moisture damage that is expensive to repair. Our plumber connects the appliance, checks every connection with an on-site test cycle, and only then considers the job complete.

Connecting a washing machine to the drain

A washing machine's drain hose is connected to the drain in one of two ways: to the sink trap through a branch, or to a separate outlet in the wall with its own water seal. A separate outlet is safer, because the machine's water doesn't flow through the sink and smells don't come back.

  • The hose is run in a loop at the height the manufacturer specifies, otherwise the machine can siphon itself empty or draw dirty water back in.
  • The hose is not stretched or kinked, and it is extended only with the manufacturer's extension.
  • The water supply needs its own shut-off valve.

If the machine is moved to the kitchen, the hallway or another spot in the bathroom, a new water supply and a drain outlet with a fall have to be built. A socket in a damp room is fitted by an electrician. Connection starts from €25, and we agree new outlets after an inspection: plumbing rough-in.

Connecting an integrated dishwasher, fridge or coffee machine

An integrated dishwasher is connected before it goes into its recess, because afterwards the connections can no longer be reached. The supply and drain hoses run through the cabinet side to the space under the sink, where the shut-off valve and the trap branch are. The machine is levelled so the door closes and the front panel sits straight.

  • A fridge with a water supply needs a thin supply line with its own valve. The line is left with some slack so the fridge can be pulled out.
  • A coffee machine plumbed into the water supply gets a valve and the supply line the manufacturer requires.
  • A small water heater (boiler) under the sink is no exception: it also needs a safety valve and a separate socket, fitted by an electrician.

After connection, every joint is tested with water. If you are fitting a small water heater where there is no hot water, see also water heater installation. We agree the price before the work.
