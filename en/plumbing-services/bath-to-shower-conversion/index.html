Bath to shower conversion in Riga

Replacing a bath with a shower is more than carrying the bath out: in its place you need a shower from which the water drains with the right fall and does not stay on the floor. We do the plumbing part: removing the old bath, adapting the drain outlet, and installing the tray or floor drain, the trap and the shower mixer. There is no single price for this job, because it depends on the chosen solution, and we agree the price before the work starts.

The main question: where will the water go

A bath stands on feet and its trap is above the floor, so the drain opening in the wall is often too high for a low shower. A shower tray is much lower, but with a floor drain the pipe has to run under the floor with a continuous fall to the riser.

So the solution is chosen by how low the outlet can go:

  • Shower with a standard tray: the tray sits on feet or a plinth, with the trap under it. This option works almost always, including with the existing outlet.
  • Low tray: only a few centimetres above the floor. It needs a lower outlet, and the tee at the riser often has to be lowered.
  • Floor drain (point or linear): a shower without a tray, with the floor sloping to the drain. The drain and the pipe under it need space, so the floor in the shower area is often raised or a plinth is built.

If the tee has to be lowered or a riser section replaced, this affects the building's common property. This work is agreed with the house manager, and the riser has to be shut off for it: riser replacement.

Sources: Section 3, Paragraph 2 and Section 4, Paragraph 1 of the Apartment Property Law (Dzīvokļa īpašuma likums), likumi.lv; LV portāls e-consultation of 24.10.2024 on risers as common property. Checked 05.11.2026.

What is plumbing work and what is not

A shower in place of a bath involves several trades. The plumber is responsible for the water and drainage, while the floor, waterproofing and tiles are finishing work done by a tiler or a renovation team.

  • Plumbing part: removing the bath, rerouting or lowering the outlet, installing the floor drain or tray and the trap, installing the shower mixer or system, and fitting the glass screen and grab rails.
  • Finishing part: building the floor slope or plinth, waterproofing the floor and walls in the shower area, tiling, and making good the wall where the bath stood.

Waterproofing is applied once the floor drain and pipes are in place, and before the tiles. The floor drain must have a waterproofing flange or collar that is bonded into the waterproofing layer. This is exactly the spot that most often starts letting water through to the neighbours, so the order of the plumbing and finishing work is agreed in advance.

Level access shower for older people

For an older person, stepping over the edge of a bath is hard and a slipping risk. A shower without a threshold or with a very low tray lets you walk in without stepping up, even with a walking frame. Such a bathroom is usually planned with:

  • a floor drain or a low tray, so there is no step;
  • grab rails on the wall fixed into masonry or built-in mounting plates, not just plasterboard;
  • a folding seat in the shower;
  • a thermostatic mixer, so the water does not suddenly turn hot;
  • a hand shower on a rail that can also be adjusted while seated;
  • non-slip tiles (a finishing choice).

The positions of the grab rails and seat are best planned before tiling, so the wall has a firm base there. For choosing the mixer see shower system installation.

How a bath to shower conversion works

  1. 1

    Inspection and choice

    The outlet height, the distance to the riser and the floor construction are measured, and the tray or floor drain is chosen on that basis.

  2. 2

    Removing the bath

    The bath is disconnected from the trap and the mixer and carried out. For the details of removal see bath replacement.

  3. 3

    Drainage and water

    The drain outlet is rerouted or lowered, and the mixer outlets are moved to the height the shower needs.

  4. 4

    Finishing work

    The tiler builds the floor slope or plinth and applies the waterproofing and tiles.

  5. 5

    Installation

    The tray or the floor drain grate, the trap, the mixer or shower system, the glass screen and the grab rails are installed.

  6. 6

    Testing

    We check that water from the whole shower area runs to the drain, that no puddles are left anywhere and that the joints are dry.

How much does a bath to shower conversion cost?

There is no single starting price for replacing a bath with a shower: the amount of work for a shower with a tray on the existing outlet and a shower with a floor drain differs several times over. We give the price for the plumbing part after an inspection and agree it before the work starts. It depends on:

  • a shower with a tray or a tiled shower with a floor drain;
  • whether the drain outlet has to be moved and the tee lowered;
  • an exposed or concealed shower mixer;
  • the type of glass screen or door;
  • carrying out an old cast iron bath.

The plumbing price does not include materials (tray, floor drain, trap, mixer, glass screen, grab rails) or taking away and disposing of the old bath. The floor slope or plinth, waterproofing, tiling and making good the wall are separate finishing jobs, and they are quoted by whoever does them.

If you are still undecided, see the article bath or shower. If you are replanning the bathroom more widely, combining the bathroom and toilet will be useful. All jobs are on the plumbing services page.
