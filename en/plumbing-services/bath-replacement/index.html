Bath replacement and installation in Riga

Bath replacement means two jobs: removing the old bath and installing the new one with a trap, an overflow and a watertight joint at the wall. Installing a new bath costs from €80, removing the old bath from €30. We give the exact price after assessing the situation and agree it before the work starts.

What matters in a bath replacement

Replacing a bath looks simple, but most leaks through to the neighbours start right at the trap and the joint between the bath and the wall. That is why these spots are the most important part of the job.

  • Bath type: an acrylic bath needs a sturdy frame or feet, a steel bath is light but noisy, a cast iron bath is very heavy.
  • Size: the new bath has to fit the recess in length and width, and its waste has to line up with the drain opening or be adapted with a flexible pipe.
  • Trap and overflow: the old ones are kept only if they are new and fit the new bath.
  • Mixer tap: if it is on the wall, the bath is positioned to suit it; if it is moved, the job is bigger.
  • Bath panel: it is fitted after the connections are made, leaving access to the trap.

Removing the old bath

Removing an old bath costs from €30. An acrylic or steel bath is disconnected from the trap and lifted out. A cast iron bath is a different story: it is heavy, often needs several people to carry or has to be broken up before it is taken out, and in an apartment building without a lift that is a separate job.

If the bath is built in or tiles have been laid over it, the edges have to be freed first. After that the bottom of the wall usually needs repair, and that is not plumbing work.

Taking the bath away and disposal are not included in the removal. Old cast iron baths are often collected free of charge by scrap metal collectors who advertise online, so it is worth arranging the removal before the work starts.

How bath installation works

  1. 1

    Shutting off the water and removal

    The apartment valves are closed, the mixer hose is removed, the trap is disconnected and the old bath is carried out.

  2. 2

    Preparing the space

    The floor and the drain opening are cleaned, the pipe is checked for damage and the position of the new bath is measured.

  3. 3

    Frame or feet

    The bath feet or frame are fitted and the bath is adjusted so that the water runs to the waste and the bath does not move.

  4. 4

    Trap and overflow

    The new trap with its overflow is connected to the drain and checked with a full bath for leaks.

  5. 5

    Joint and panel

    The joint between the bath and the wall is sealed and, if agreed, a panel is fitted with access to the trap.

For a bath replacement, closing the apartment valves is usually enough. If a valve does not close, the water has to be shut off at the riser, and the house manager arranges that.

Bath or shower?

In many small bathrooms a shower goes in place of the bath. That is a different job: a shower cabin or shower area needs a tray or floor drain with the right fall to the drain. For this see the shower cabin installation page, price from €80.

If you are replacing all the bathroom fittings and pipes, it is easier to plan them together: bathroom plumbing from €200. If you are also replacing the toilet at the same time, see toilet installation. All jobs are on the plumbing services page.

Replacing a cast-iron bath with an acrylic or steel bath

An old cast-iron bath is most often replaced with an acrylic or steel one. An acrylic bath is light and warm, but it needs a sturdy frame, otherwise it flexes and the seal along the wall cracks. A steel bath is cheaper and more rigid, but louder; it stands on feet, and sound-deadening material can be applied underneath.

The new bath has to fit where the old one was. In Soviet-era bathrooms the alcove is often exactly the length of the old bath, and a new standard-size bath can be a few centimetres longer or shorter. Measure the alcove at the walls, not the old bath, and check that the wall-mounted mixer tap will stay at the right height.

The joint between the bath and the wall is sealed once the bath has been filled, so it can settle: bath sealing. If you are thinking of a shower instead of the bath, see bath or shower.

Installing corner, freestanding and whirlpool baths

  • Corner bath: needs a frame and two walls meeting at a right angle. If the walls are out of true, the joint will be wide.
  • Freestanding bath: the waste outlet must be in the floor directly under the bath outlet, and the mixer tap goes on the floor or the wall. This is planned before the floor is tiled.
  • Whirlpool bath: needs a separate electrical supply with residual current protection, installed by an electrician, and a hatch in the bath panel to reach the pump.

For any bath, the feet are adjusted so the water runs towards the outlet and the bath does not move. The panel under the bath gets an access hatch at the trap, because the trap is one of the most common leak points.

Bath installation starts from €80, but for more complex baths we agree the price after an inspection, because it depends on the frame, the outlet position and the connections. If a shower will take the place of the bath, see bath to shower conversion.
