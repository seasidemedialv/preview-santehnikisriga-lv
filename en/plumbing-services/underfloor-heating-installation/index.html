Why underfloor heating is a natural fit for a bathroom renovation

If you're already planning a bathroom or floor renovation - changing the tiles, pulling up the old floor covering, or connecting new fixtures - that's the best moment to build in a water underfloor heating system. Laying the pipework happens before the new floor covering goes down, and connecting it to your existing heating system or manifold is technically similar to other plumbing work.

We work with water (hydronic) underfloor heating - not electric heating mats, which fall under an electrician's scope. The work covers preparing the subfloor, laying the pipe loops, connecting the system and a pressure test before the floor covering goes down, to make sure everything works safely.

How underfloor heating installation works

From subfloor preparation to a finished system ready for tiling

1

Subfloor preparation

We clean and level the subfloor, adding insulation if needed

2

Pipe laying

We lay the pipe loops in a pattern suited to the room

3

Manifold connection

We connect the system to your existing heating system or heat source

4

Pressure testing

We test the system before the floor covering goes down - no leaks

Why correct installation matters right before tiling

The underfloor heating pipework stays under the floor covering - if a leak or a bad connection turns up after tiling, fixing it means breaking up the floor. That is why a pressure test is done before covering, and the connection to the manifold or heating system is checked. Careful planning and testing before the floor goes down is the only way to avoid costly repairs down the line.
