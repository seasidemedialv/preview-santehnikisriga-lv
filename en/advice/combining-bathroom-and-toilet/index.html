Combining the bathroom and toilet in an apartment

Combining the bathroom and toilet is a change to the room layout, and it must be approved by the building authority before the work starts, because without documents it is unauthorised construction. If only the non-load-bearing partition is removed and the fixtures stay above the same kind of rooms on the floor below, the process is simpler. If the bathroom is moved elsewhere, above a neighbour's living room, the requirements are stricter. The plumbing work starts after approval, and the risers remain the building's common property.

Does combining need approval

Yes. An LV portāls explanation about an apartment where the previous owner combined the toilet and bathroom without documents states: if rooms have been combined or divided in the layout, you need documents prepared by an architect, which must be approved by the building authority. After that, the cadastral data must be updated with the State Land Service.

Unapproved work is unauthorised construction under the Construction Law (Būvniecības likums). For a private individual the fine can be up to 400 fine units. Unregistered changes may also affect home insurance and later the sale of the apartment or a loan.

Source: LV portāls e-consultation of 25.11.2022 (Section 19, Paragraph 3 and Section 25, Paragraph 1, Clause 5 of the Construction Law). The explanation is for information only. Checked on 05.11.2026.

Which document you need: notification, explanatory memorandum or building design

Under the Building Regulations (Ēku būvnoteikumi), the document depends on the scope of the work:

  • notification of construction: simplified renovation, when functional improvements are made in the rooms without changing the load-bearing structures, the building volume, the type of use or the facade;
  • explanatory memorandum: simplified alteration, when openings are made or changed in load-bearing structures, including utility shafts or penetrations;
  • building design and building permit: in all other cases.

Whether the partition between the toilet and bathroom is load-bearing and which document applies to your apartment is decided by an architect based on the building design or a survey. The documents are submitted through the construction information system, and they are reviewed by the institution acting as the building authority. An interior design without alteration or renovation work does not need documents.

Source: Cabinet Regulation No. 529 of 02.09.2014 "Building Regulations", Sub-clauses 1.¹ 5 and 1.¹ 6 (definitions), Clauses 7, 7.¹, 7.² and 7.³ (likumi.lv). Checked on 05.11.2026.

The neighbour below and the co-owners

The building code on general requirements for structures, LBN 200-21, states that in an apartment building sanitary rooms are placed above rooms of a similar purpose on the floor below. They are permitted above the living rooms of the apartment below if the requirements for sound insulation, waterproofing, thermal insulation, fire safety, ventilation and safe use are met.

In 2020 a specialist at the Riga building authority explained that when a bathroom is placed above the living rooms of the apartment below, the documentation must be agreed with the owner of the apartment below, and that owner often does not agree. If the bathroom is enlarged at the expense of the hallway or another utility room, the co-owners' consent is not needed, but the work must be approved by the building authority. At that time the previous building code was in force, so check the specific requirement with the building authority.

If the work affects common property (risers, load-bearing walls or the ventilation shaft), you need the consent of the owners' meeting.

Sources: Cabinet Regulation No. 693 of 19.10.2021, LBN 200-21, Clauses 3.31 and 136 (likumi.lv); LV portāls of 15.12.2020 (D. Skalbe, representative of the Riga City Building Authority, in a webinar); Section 4 and Section 16, Paragraph 2, Clause 2.¹ of the Apartment Property Law (Dzīvokļa īpašuma likums) (likumi.lv). Checked on 05.11.2026.

The risers stay in place and accessible

The pipes up to the common risers belong to the apartment, but the risers belong to the whole building. They are usually left where they are, and the new fixtures are connected to them. Moving the risers is a matter for the owners' meeting, not for one owner.

If the pipes are hidden in boxing or behind tiles, access hatches must be left opposite the cleanouts: this is required by the building code LBN 221-15. Shutting off the riser during the work is organised by the house manager, and it is often a paid service according to the house manager's price list. If the risers are old cast iron or steel, consider riser replacement before tiling.

Sources: Section 3, Paragraph 2 and Section 4 of the Apartment Property Law (likumi.lv); Clauses 239.2 and 253 of LBN 221-15 (Cabinet Regulation No. 332 of 30.06.2015, likumi.lv). Checked on 05.11.2026.

Plumbing work: outlets, fall and connections

  1. 1

    Fixture plan

    Draw where the toilet, sink, bath or shower and washing machine will be, and how far each fixture is from the sewer riser. The toilet is usually placed closest to the riser, because it has the largest pipe.

  2. 2

    Waste outlets and fall

    Waste pipe runs are built straight, and changing the pipe gradient between connections is prohibited. The further a fixture is from the riser, the higher the pipe has to start, so a shower placed far away may need a raised floor. Fixtures from two different apartments must not be connected to one shared waste pipe.

  3. 3

    Shower without a tray

    The floor in the shower area must slope at 10–20 ‰ towards the floor drain or channel. A floor drain is not mandatory in an apartment bathroom.

  4. 4

    Water supply

    The new pipes are run in the wall or in boxing, with a shut-off valve for each branch. Before closing up, the system is pressure tested and the pipe layout is photographed.

  5. 5

    Waterproofing and ventilation

    Before tiling, the floor and shower area are waterproofed. The ventilation shaft is common property and must not be built over.

This stage, before the tiler starts work, is called plumbing rough-in. Once the tiles are done, the fixtures are installed: this is described on the page about bathroom plumbing.

Source: Clauses 227, 228, 232 and 235 of LBN 221-15 (Cabinet Regulation No. 332 of 30.06.2015, likumi.lv); Clause 136 of LBN 200-21. Checked on 05.11.2026.
