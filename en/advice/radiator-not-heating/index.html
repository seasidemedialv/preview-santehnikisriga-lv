Radiator not heating: what to check and who to tell

A radiator is most often not heating because air has built up in it, the thermostatic valve is stuck or a valve is closed, or the heating has not yet been switched on in the building. If one radiator is cold but the others are warm, the fault is usually at that radiator, and it can be fixed in the apartment. If all the radiators are cold, or the neighbours on the same riser have the same problem, it is a matter for the building's heating system and must be reported to the house manager.

First: one radiator or the whole building

Before you turn anything, feel all the radiators in the apartment and the pipes that lead to them. Also ask the neighbours above and below you. The table helps you work out where the fault most likely is.

Has the heating season started for your building

In an apartment building the heating is switched on for the whole building, not for a single apartment. In 2025 Rīgas namu pārvaldnieks explained its criteria: for at least three days the air has not warmed above 14 degrees, at some point it has cooled to 8 degrees, and similar weather is expected for the next three days. Each building is told its switch-on date individually on the e-parvaldnieks.lv portal or in the app.

For the first two weeks after switch-on the systems are stabilised and bled, so some radiators may warm up unevenly at first. Apartment owners can agree on an earlier or later switch-on if there is a person authorised in advance to choose the date on behalf of the building. Other house managers may have different arrangements, so ask yours.

In mild weather the radiators may be cool during the day even after switch-on. In 2019 RNP stated that an automatic heating substation supplies heat to the apartments when the outdoor temperature drops below +12 degrees.

Sources: LSM of 30.09.2025 on the Rīgas namu pārvaldnieks criteria for starting the heating season and the stabilisation period; LV portāls of 27.09.2019 on heating substation controls. Arrangements may differ in each building. Checked on 05.11.2026.

What you can safely check yourself

  1. 1

    Thermostatic head to maximum

    Turn the thermostatic head to the highest number and wait half an hour. If the room is warm, the thermostatic head is closing the radiator correctly, and that is not a fault.

  2. 2

    Check the valves

    If the radiator has a hand valve, make sure it is open. Do not touch a valve with a cap and an adjusting screw under it: it is set for the whole system.

  3. 3

    The thermostatic valve pin

    Remove the thermostatic head (it is usually held by a nut or a clip). Under it there is a metal pin. If it is pressed in and does not spring back out, it has stuck over the summer. Press it lightly and release it a few times. If water appears around the pin, stop and put the head back on.

  4. 4

    Bleeding

    The bleed valve is in the top corner of the radiator. Hold a cloth and a container underneath and slowly turn the screw with a screwdriver or a radiator key. When the air stops hissing and water appears, close it. The water may be hot and dark, so do not take the screw all the way out.

  5. 5

    Free space for the heat

    Curtains, furniture and a thick layer of dust reduce the heat output. Rīgas siltums recommends keeping radiators clear and vacuuming them before the season.

If the radiator leaks at a valve or joint, do not force old nuts tighter. Put a container underneath, wipe up the water and report it to the house manager or book a repair.

Sources: LSM of 05.03.2026 (RTU researcher A. Zajacs on bleeding and sludge at the bottom of the radiator); Rīgas siltums frequently asked questions (rs.lv). Checked on 05.11.2026.

When it is the house manager's job

The building's heating system is common property. Under the Apartment Property Law (Dzīvokļa īpašuma likums), the radiators in an apartment also belong to the common property if their operation depends on the building's utilities. Maintenance and routine repair of the system are a mandatory duty of the house manager under the Residential Property Management Law (Dzīvojamo māju pārvaldīšanas likums).

Report it to the house manager if all the radiators are cold, if air builds up again after bleeding or if several apartments on one riser are cold. Then the fault is in the heating substation, in removing air from the riser or in balancing. Another frequent cause is a radiator that someone has replaced without approval: read about this in the article on getting radiator replacement approved.

In your report, give the address, which radiators are not heating, since when, and whether the neighbours have the same problem. RNP asks for faults to be reported on the e-parvaldnieks.lv portal or in the app.

Sources: Section 4, Paragraph 1, Clause 1 of the Apartment Property Law (likumi.lv); Section 6, Paragraph 2 of the Residential Property Management Law (likumi.lv); LSM of 30.09.2025. Checked on 05.11.2026.

When to order a radiator repair

Radiator repair is needed if the fault is at your radiator but you cannot fix it yourself:

  • the thermostatic valve pin will not free up or the valve drips;
  • the bleed valve is painted over, broken off or will not turn;
  • the shut-off valve leaks or will not close;
  • you want to fit a thermostatic head or replace an old valve;
  • the radiator heats only partly even after bleeding, and the cause is sludge.

Changing a thermostatic head does not require the riser to be shut off. To replace a valve or a radiator in a building with central heating, the riser usually has to be closed and drained, and the house manager organises this. Rīgas siltums states that radiators can be replaced from about 1 May to 1 October, once the technical documentation has been approved.

If the radiator is old and clogged, radiator replacement may be the better option, which costs from €70 per radiator (labour). Repair costs from €40 per call-out, and the final price depends on the type of valves, access and whether the riser has to be shut off. We agree the price before the work starts.
