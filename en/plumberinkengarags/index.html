Plumber in Ķengarags, Riga

A plumber in Ķengarags works in one of Riga's largest apartment estates, where five-storey blocks and twelve-storey towers were built to a 1962 plan. Most of the work here is in apartment pipes, bathrooms and risers, which in many places have been in service since the buildings went up. This page describes what tends to break in these buildings and what the price depends on.

Five-storey blocks and twelve-storey towers: different risks

Multi-storey construction in Ķengarags began under a detailed plan of 1962, and the estate grew out of five microdistricts. Rows of five-storey blocks are joined by twelve-storey tower buildings. The district sources do not name specific building series, so we look at the pipe layout of each building on site.

In a five-storey block one riser serves five apartments stacked one above the other. In a tower building the same riser has more than twice as many apartments: a leak at the top can run several floors down, and shutting off the riser affects many neighbours at once. That is why in a tower the timing of riser work is agreed especially carefully.

Blockages behave differently too. If the shared sewer stack blocks at the bottom, water from the upper floors rises in the bath or toilet of the lowest apartments. Residents of the ground and first floors notice it first.

Risers as common property and the service life of pipes

For water and sewer pipes, Annex 2 of Cabinet Regulation No. 906 provides a service life of 15–30 years depending on the material, and 60 years for sewerage with cast iron fittings. In buildings put up in the 1960s and later, pipes that have not been replaced have mostly already exceeded this period.

A riser belongs to all the apartment owners in the building together, so the decision on new risers and how to pay for them is taken by a vote of the owners' meeting. A single apartment owner cannot decide to replace it through all the floors, but can raise the question and renew the branch pipes up to the riser in their own apartment. How such a decision is made and how the money is shared is described in the article who pays for riser replacement.

Riser replacement within one floor costs from €100 per riser, excluding materials. The work costs more if an old metal section has to be cut out, if the boxing is tiled, or if the cold and hot water pipes and the sewer pipe all have to be replaced at once.

Standard service periods from Cabinet Regulation No. 906 (Annex 2), as summarised by LV portāls; the owners' decision on risers under the Apartment Property Law (Dzīvokļa īpašuma likums, Sections 3 and 16) in the LV portāls explanation of 24.10.2024. Checked 05.11.2026.

The bathroom before and after riser replacement

In Ķengarags apartments the risers are usually in boxing or a recess in the bathroom or toilet. If the building is planning riser replacement, it makes sense to plan new tiles and a new bath after it, so that the boxing does not have to be opened twice. If no replacement is planned, leave an access hatch in the boxing at the riser joints.

  • Bath replacement in Ķengarags: from €80; removal of the old bath from €30.
  • Toilet replacement: from €60; the price depends on the outlet type and whether the old toilet is cemented into the floor.
  • Shower cabin installation: from €80, if you choose a shower instead of a bath and the outlet height allows it.

The Southern Bridge side: shallow groundwater

Geological conditions in Ķengarags are generally fairly good, and the ground surface is nowhere lower than 4 m above sea level, because the district sits on the high bank of the Daugava. The exception is the north-western part from the Southern Bridge (Dienvidu tilts) to Ķengaraga iela, where groundwater is shallower than 1.5 m.

In this part of the district, building basements are more often damp, and the shared sewer and inlets run through them. In a damp setting metal pipes and clamps rust faster, so the condition of the basement networks shows well how urgently the building needs to think about renewing its risers and mains.

Water is leaking to the neighbours: first steps

  1. 1

    Shut off the water in the apartment

    If the leak is beyond the apartment valve, this will stop it. If it is before the valve or the riser itself, tell the house manager at once.

  2. 2

    Warn the neighbours below

    In a tower building the water can reach several floors down, and they need to check their ceilings and electrics.

    Send a request
