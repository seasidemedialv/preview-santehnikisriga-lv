Plumber in Old Riga

A plumber in Old Riga works in buildings dating from the Middle Ages, the Renaissance and the Baroque, and the walls of these houses decide where pipes can run. About 2,000 people live in the district, but many buildings contain public and commercial premises, so the job is often not in an apartment. Here we explain what complicates plumbing in the Old Town and who is responsible for what.

Thick walls and pipes that follow the existing route

The Old Town (Vecpilsēta), as the district is officially called, was added to the UNESCO list in 1998 together with the rest of the historic centre. Its masonry buildings have thick walls, and cutting a new route through them is expensive and not always possible. So the water supply and sewerage usually run through existing shafts, recesses or along a wall.

A new sink, toilet or shower can only be placed where a sewer fall to the existing outlet can be achieved. If the room is far from the riser, the solution may be a raised floor or a sewage pump, but a pump is chosen only if gravity drainage is not possible.

Basements in made ground

Building conditions in Old Riga are difficult: the groundwater lies at 1.5–3 m, and the top layer is made ground several metres thick, fill built up over the centuries. Thanks to the reinforced embankment there is no real flood threat.

The basement, where the building's sewer outlet and water inlet run, tends to be damp, and old pipes there are exposed to corrosion. If a toilet or kitchen has been fitted in a basement lower than the street sewer, the waste water has to be pumped up. Such a pump must be checked regularly, because if it stops, the room floods.

Catering and public premises: sewerage under load

Public buildings dominate the north-western part of the Old Town, while central mixed-use areas cover 24.2 ha. In commercial premises plumbing carries a different load than in an apartment: toilets are used by many people, and fat and food waste go down kitchen drains.

  • Fat cools in the drainpipe and sticks to the walls, so a kitchen outlet clogs up gradually until at some point the water no longer drains.
  • In a heavily used toilet the cistern mechanism wears out faster, and a leaking cistern quietly wastes a lot of water.
  • If the building's common outlet is blocked, it affects everyone using the building, so the work is agreed with the building owner or house manager.

Rīgas ūdens is responsible only up to the building's meter chamber or the building's outer wall. If a pipe bursts or the sewer is blocked inside the building, the repair is the building owner's responsibility, even if the premises are leased. How costs are shared with the tenant is set by the lease.

Apartments in the Old Town: what is yours and what is the building's

Section 3(2) of the Apartment Property Law (Dzīvokļa īpašuma likums) states that an apartment includes the utility lines up to the common property risers, while Section 4 counts the building's service systems as common property. In an old building, where the risers have been rebuilt several times and joined in unusual places, this boundary is not always obvious.

If it is unclear where your pipes connect to the building's network, we find out before the work. This is also useful when buying or selling an apartment: a pre-sale plumbing inspection costs from €60 and shows the condition of the supply pipes, taps and drains.

Water is leaking into a shop or café below

In Old Town buildings, apartments and offices are often above shops and catering premises. A leak on an upper floor here affects not only a neighbour's ceiling but also someone else's business, its goods and equipment, so you have to act at once.

  1. 1

    Stop the water

    Close the valve in your premises. If the leak is before it or in the building's common pipe, report it to the house manager or owner.

  2. 2

    Warn the premises below

    So they have time to switch off the electricity and move goods or equipment.

  3. 3

    Record the damage

    Photos and a description will be useful for the insurer and in dealings between landlord and tenant; documenting plumbing work after flooding costs from €70.
