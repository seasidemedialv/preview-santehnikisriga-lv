Plumber in Jugla, Riga

A plumber in Jugla works in two different settings: a large-panel estate from the 1960s with khrushchyovka blocks, and private-house quarters where city sewerage only arrived in some streets in 2024–2025. Below is what is typical of each and what you need to know before connecting to the new network.

Khrushchyovkas of silicate brick and panels

In the 1960s one of Riga's first large-panel estates, with three microdistricts, was built in Jugla. Some of the buildings are made of silicate brick, while the rest of the microdistrict housing is dominated by five-storey panel khrushchyovkas. Private houses began to be built in the district as early as the 1950s.

In a khrushchyovka the bathroom and toilet are often combined in a small room, and the risers run right through it. You can replace the bath with a shower or fit a new toilet, but first you need to check where and at what height the sewer outlet is, because a shower tray needs a fall to the riser.

Fixings also differ between brick and panel buildings. A heavy water heater (boiler) or a wall-hung toilet needs different anchors in a brick wall than in a concrete panel, and an ordinary wall plug may not hold in hollow silicate bricks. We check this before drilling.

New sewerage between Šmerļa mežs and the Strazdupīte

In the private-house quarter between Šmerļa mežs (Šmerlis forest) and the Strazdupīte stream there was water supply in many places, but no sewerage. Waste water from the houses went into local systems, and pollution reached the Strazdupīte and Lake Jugla. In 2024–2025 Rīgas ūdens built about 2 km of water mains, more than 4 km of sewers and 2 pumping stations there for more than 140 households.

If the network in your street has been handed over but the house still uses a septic tank or holding tank, Cabinet Regulation No. 384 requires you to connect if the local system does not meet environmental requirements. Until the connection is built, the local system must be registered with the Housing and Environment Department under Riga Binding Regulation No. 66.

Inside the house the work is to prepare one outlet for the new connection pipe, check the fall of the internal sewerage and disconnect the pipe to the old tank. In a house from the 1950s the kitchen and bathroom often drain to different places; they are joined before the switch-over.

Network construction in 2024–2025: Rīgas ūdens, “Development of the Jugla water supply networks”. Obligation to connect: Cabinet Regulation No. 384 (as explained by Rīgas ūdens). Registration: Riga Binding Regulation No. 66 (likumi.lv). Checked 05.11.2026.

The shores of Ķīšezers and Lake Jugla: peat and shallow groundwater

In the north-western part between Ķīšezers and Pakalniešu iela, and by Lake Jugla, groundwater is shallower than 1.5 m, and the ground contains peat deposits. In a wide strip along the shore the flood risk is up to 10%.

  • A private-house basement by the lake may need a pump that works for much of the year; if it switches off too late or runs non-stop, the float and the non-return valve are checked.
  • A sewer connection pipe laid in peat can settle, and a dip forms in it where sediment collects.
  • A water inlet laid shallow freezes more often in winter; thawing costs from €55.

Cloudy water after trunk main flushing

When flushing the trunk water main, Rīgas ūdens warned of possible cloudy water specifically in Jugla and Berģi. The water clears afterwards, but some of the sediment stays in the building's network.

The signs are a weak stream from one tap, a hissing water heater or a washing machine error about the water supply. You can clean the aerator and the machine's inlet screen yourself; if the pressure does not recover, low water pressure diagnosis costs from €25.

Private houses from the 1950s: an old inlet and weak pressure

In Jugla private houses built since the 1950s, water supply has been in place for a long time in many places, so the inlet from the street to the house may be very old. Over the years a steel pipe clogs with rust from the inside, and the pressure in the house drops even though it is normal in the street.

If the water runs weakly from all the taps but the neighbours have no problem, the cause is usually in your inlet or the house's internal pipes. Before replacing the inlet, it is worth checking whether the fault is a clogged screen or a partly closed valve at the inlet, because that costs much less.
